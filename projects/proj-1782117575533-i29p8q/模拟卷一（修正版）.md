China's GDP grew by 2.3 percent year on year to hit 101.6 trillion yuan in 2020, surpassing the 100 trillion yuan threshold. From a 6.8-percent contraction in the first quarter, to 3.2-percent expansion in the second, 4.9-percent recovery in the third and 6.5-percent rebound in the fourth, the V-shaped trajectory made China the only major economy to log positive growth in that year.

**Passage 2: International Trade Types**

There are many different types of international trade. The most basic division is between visible trade and invisible trade. Visible trade refers to trade in physical goods, while invisible trade is trade in services. When China exports televisions to the United States, that is visible trade. When a Chinese company provides shipping services for a foreign buyer, that is invisible trade.

Another common type is processing trade. In processing trade, Chinese companies import raw materials or parts from other countries, process or assemble them into finished products, and then export the finished products. This type of trade has been very important for China's economic development over the past decades. There are two forms: processing with imported materials, where Chinese firms buy the materials and then export the finished products, and processing with supplied materials, where the foreign supplier provides the materials and Chinese firms just do the processing.

**Passage 2**

The World Trade Organization (WTO) is the only global international organization dealing with the rules of trade between nations. At its heart are the WTO agreements, negotiated and signed by the majority of the world's trading nations. The goal is to help producers of goods and services, exporters, and importers conduct their business.

Two of the most important principles of the WTO are most-favored-nation treatment and national treatment. Most-favored-nation means treating all trading partners equally — if you give a special favor to one country, you have to give it to all WTO members. National treatment means treating imported goods the same as domestically produced goods once they have entered the market.

The WTO system aims to make trade free and predictable, and to promote fair and undistorted competition. It allows member countries to negotiate trade agreements and settle trade disputes through a standardized process.
